# Sources and validation for the deformation and intersection notes

## Audience, purpose and scope

These notes assume the scheme theory, sheaves, morphisms, projective constructions and differentials of Hartshorne, Chapters I–II, together with elementary commutative algebra. They introduce flat families and infinitesimal deformations before building the normal cone and deformation to that cone. Cycles, Chow groups, Chern operations and Gysin maps are then introduced to explain what the construction does for intersections.

The central route is algebraic and explicit: powers of an ideal, conormal modules, associated graded rings, ordinary and extended Rees algebras, affine deformation equations, blow-up charts, specialization and intersection classes. General theorems beyond the assumed background are identified as cited tools. The notes do not require a prior course in moduli theory, derived geometry, obstruction complexes or virtual fundamental classes.

The document has 40 pages in the current build, with sixteen exercises and worked solutions. Appendix A, pages 21–22, records conventions, cited tools and a reading route. Appendix B, pages 23–29, contains 64 glossary definitions and a notation table. Appendix C, **Examples Glossary**, pages 30–40, contains two examples for each definition, giving 128 examples with links in both directions.

## Conventions and source pitfalls

- The ground field is $k=\mathbb C$ unless explicitly stated otherwise. This makes the elementary length, tangent and coordinate-change examples unambiguous.
- The **ordinary Rees algebra** is the nonnegatively graded algebra used for a blow-up. The **extended Rees algebra** $A[t,I/t]\subset A[t,t^{-1}]$ defines the deformation over the genuine base parameter $t$. Its quotient by $t$ is the associated graded algebra, not the original ring with the ideal merely set to zero.
- The cone is placed at $t=0$. Fulton's full-book global construction places it at infinity; a change of coordinate on the parameter line reconciles the presentations.
- Presenting the extended algebra using generators and naive equations can create parameter torsion. The construction inside the Laurent ring, or saturation by the parameter, gives the actual closure of the general family. The nonreduced examples retain their nilpotents throughout.
- The total space of a locally free sheaf $E$ is $\operatorname{Spec}\operatorname{Sym}(E^\vee)$. The conormal sheaf is $I/I^2$, while the normal sheaf is its dual. The normal cone is an honest vector bundle in the regular-embedding case. The Stacks Project sometimes uses “vector bundle” for a more general symmetric-algebra construction; the notes explicitly use finite locally free sheaves for honest vector bundles.
- Projective bundles parameterize **lines**: $\mathbb P(E)=\operatorname{Proj}\operatorname{Sym}(E^\vee)$. A cone with graded algebra $S$ has completion $\operatorname{Proj}S[z]$ and boundary $\operatorname{Proj}S$. Stating these algebras avoids ambiguity with Hartshorne's quotient convention.
- With the Rees grading, $\mathcal O(1)=\mathcal O(-E)$ on the blow-up. Thus $\mathcal O(E)|_E=\mathcal O_E(-1)$. Vakil Class 14, printed page 4, has a sign slip in one sentence identifying the effective exceptional divisor with the Rees $\mathcal O(1)$; the new notes use the correct convention.
- The excess rank is $e=r-s$, the original codimension minus the upper codimension. Vakil Class 17, printed page 4, mislabels the rank in one line while using the correct $c_e$ in the formula.
- Segre classes use the projective completion and its tautological first Chern operation. For a normal bundle $N$, the formula is $s(N)=c(N)^{-1}\cap[Z]$. Its inverse-Chern sign is distinct from the exceptional-divisor sign.
- All ordinary and refined Gysin maps lower dimension by the codimension of the **original regular embedding**. The upper inverse-image embedding may fail to be regular; its normal cone still embeds in the pulled-back original normal bundle.
- A cycle, its Chow class, and its local multiplicity are different objects. Numerical degree on Chow classes is taken on proper schemes. The exceptional curve's global numerical self-intersection is therefore illustrated on $\operatorname{Bl}_p\mathbb P^2$; the notes separately explain why its pushed zero-cycle class vanishes on the nonproper $\operatorname{Bl}_0\mathbb A^2$.
- A first-order embedded motion may be abstractly trivial. The normal sheaf classifies the embedded problem; it should not be silently substituted for the deformation theory of the abstract scheme. The elementary obstruction example concerns lifting a parameter map and is described as that specific lifting problem.

## Direct calculations and general theorems

Direct calculations include local tangency lengths, dual-number equations, the simplest embedded deformation spaces, regular and nonregular cone examples, both Rees presentations, the affine origin and doubled-Cartier deformation models, saturation, the tangency family $tv=x^2$, the two blow-up charts and the exceptional normal-line sign. These examples make the mechanisms visible with polynomial rings and modules.

Theorems used without complete general proofs include the flatness criteria, projective flat-family Hilbert-polynomial constancy, the general classification of embedded first-order deformations, the affine hypersurface (T^1) result, the regular-sequence associated-graded theorem, general blow-up gluing, Chow operations, vector-bundle homotopy invariance, specialization respecting rational equivalence, Chern and Segre theory, and refined Gysin, excess and self-intersection. Appendix A lists these tools and gives a reading route. Explaining examples does not claim to prove these general results from Hartshorne I–II alone.

The self-intersection theorem is valid for the stated regular embedding; it does not impose an unstated smoothness assumption on the centre. The preceding excess fundamental-class statement is deliberately presented first in a smooth special case.
